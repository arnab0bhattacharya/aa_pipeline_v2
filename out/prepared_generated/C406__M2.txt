The rain in Valdor did not fall so much as it hung in the air, a grey curtain that smelled of wet stone and ozone. For forty years, Elías Thorne had managed the Grand Hotel Meridional, standing at the threshold of history with a towel draped over his shoulder and a ledger in his hand. He had served Presidents who signed treaties with trembling hands and rebels who signed ultimatums with bleeding fingers. The room keys were the same brass; the towels were the same white; the only difference was the weight of the secret held by the spine of the guest.

Tonight, the weight was crushing.

It began at four o’clock, when General Valerius Cruz, the iron-fisted head of the National Guard, entered the lobby. He was a man composed of sharp angles and dark wool, his eyes scanning the room not for comfort, but for threats. He requested the entire third floor. "I require privacy, Thorne," Cruz said, his voice a low rumble like distant thunder. "My staff will take the east wing. I will take Suite 304. No one is to disturb me until dawn."

Elías nodded, his face a mask of practiced neutrality. He handed over the key, a small brass rectangle that felt heavy in his palm. Cruz did not thank him. He merely turned and ascended the marble staircase, his boots echoing like gunshots in the quiet hall.

At six o’clock, the storm intensified. The wind battered the stained-glass windows, rattling the lead frames. Then came the second guest. He did not walk in; he was carried in by two men who looked less like servants and more like shadows. General Silas Vane, the leader of the insurrectionary junta, was a smaller man than Cruz, but his presence filled the room with a suffocating heat. He wore a coat of charcoal grey, soaked through, and his face was pale, drawn by exhaustion and fear.

"Room 305," Vane whispered. His voice was thin, barely audible over the rain. "And silence, Thorne. Absolute silence. If you speak a word of my presence to anyone, especially that man you just checked in, I will have your hotel razed."

Elías felt a cold sweat prickle at his hairline. Suite 304 and 305 were adjacent. The wall between them was thin plaster, barely a whisper away from transparency. If Cruz heard Vane’s breathing, or if Vane heard Cruz’s boots pacing, the night would end in blood. The lobby would become a battlefield, and Elías, the keeper of the house, would be the first to fall.

"Silence is our currency, General," Elías said, his voice steady. "You have your key. The east wing is quiet. I suggest you take a hot bath and rest. The capital is dangerous tonight."

Vane stared at him, searching for a lie, then nodded. He was led away, his footsteps soft, muffled by the thick carpet of the upper hallway.

Elías stood alone in the lobby, the smell of ozone stronger now. He looked up at the third floor, a dark line against the storm-lit sky. He had two hours until his shift ended, but he knew he would not be leaving. He moved to the service corridor, a narrow passageway that ran along the back of the hotel, accessible only to the staff. It was a labyrinth of shadows and steam pipes.

He climbed the stairs, his old knees protesting. As he reached the third floor, he could hear the faint sound of water running in the east wing. Vane was bathing. Elías moved quickly to the back door of Suite 304. It was locked. He had a master key, but using it would be a betrayal of trust that Cruz would never forgive. Instead, he pressed his ear to the wood. He heard nothing but the rain.

He moved to 305. The door was ajar. A sliver of light spilled into the corridor. Elías paused, his heart hammering against his ribs. He pushed the door open just enough to slip inside. The room was warm, the air thick with the scent of lavender and gun oil. Vane was sitting on the edge of the bed, a pistol in his hand, cleaning the barrel with a rag. He looked up, his eyes wide with surprise, then narrowing into slits.

"Thorne," Vane hissed. "I told you to leave me alone."

"General, I am not here to disturb you. I am here to warn you." Elías stepped further into the room, keeping his hands visible. "General Cruz is in 304. He is awake. I can hear him pacing. If you make a sound, he will know. If he knows, he will come. And you will not survive the hallway."

Vane lowered the pistol. "He is a fool. He thinks he holds the city."

"He holds the barracks," Elías said softly. "And he has men at every exit. You are trapped, General. But so is he. He is waiting for you to make a move. He is waiting for the storm to pass."

Vane looked out the window. The lightning illuminated the street below, revealing the wet cobblestones and the dark silhouettes of the city. "What do you suggest?"

"Rest," Elías said. "Sleep. The storm will break by dawn. The city will be in chaos, but it will be a chaos without blood. Let the morning decide."

Vane stared at him for a long moment. Then, slowly, he set the pistol on the nightstand. He lay back on the bed, closing his eyes. "You are a strange man, Thorne."

"I am a keeper," Elías said. "I keep the house. I keep the peace."

He stepped out, closing the door softly. He returned to the corridor and stood between the two doors. He could hear the faint sound of Cruz’s boots in 304, a rhythmic tap-tap-tap against the floorboards. He could hear the soft breathing of Vane in 305. The wall between them was a thin membrane of plaster and silence.
