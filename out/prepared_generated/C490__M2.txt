The snow in Vaelis did not fall; it drifted, a heavy, suffocating white that swallowed the spires of the Northern Kingdom whole. It was the season of silence, when the wind held its breath and the city of towers seemed to float above the frozen earth. Elara pulled her threadbare shawl tighter against her chest, her fingers numb and red as beetles. She had been walking the cobblestones of the High District for three hours, her matches unlit, her stomach a hollow ache that throbbed in time with her pulse.

She had never owned anything beautiful. Her world was made of cracked wood, soot-stained paper, and the sharp, chemical bite of sulfur. Beauty, in Elara’s experience, was something that happened to others, behind the iron gates of the palaces or in the velvet-lined windows of the merchants. It was distant, cold, and inaccessible.

That was until she saw the glint.

It lay in a patch of shadow beneath the overhang of the Palace of Aethelgard, half-buried in the pristine drift. It was a comb, but not like the wooden ones used by the laundresses. It was carved from dark bone, inlaid with veins of gold and studded with small, deep-blue stones that seemed to hold their own light even in the grey twilight. It looked like a shard of the night sky had fallen and frozen.

Elara stopped. The cold in her hands was forgotten. She knelt, the snow crunching softly under her knees, and brushed the white powder away. The comb was intact. Perfect.

Her first instinct, born of starvation, was to run to the market square. A comb like that, with gold in its teeth, could fetch enough coin for three loaves of stale bread and a cup of hot broth. It could mean the difference between shivering death in a doorway and a night of warmth. She looked around. The street was empty. The guards at the palace gates were distant silhouettes, frozen in their posts. No one had seen her.

She slipped the comb into her pocket. It felt heavy, not just in weight, but in significance. As she stood, a gust of wind tore the shawl from her shoulders, and the cold bit into her skin, sharper than before. But beneath the shivering, there was a strange, quiet hum. For the first time in her life, Elara held something that was not broken, not dirty, not used. It was hers.

She did not go to the market. Instead, she walked to the low bridge over the River Kael, where the mist rose from the black water. She sat on the edge, her legs dangling over the abyss, and pulled the comb from her pocket. The blue stones caught the faint light of the setting moon. She ran her thumb over the gold inlays, feeling the smoothness of the metal. It was warm now, warmed by the pocket, by her body.

Who had lost it? A queen? A duchess? Someone whose life was woven from silk and song? The thought made Elara feel small, yet oddly connected. To lose such a thing must be a catastrophe. To find it, a miracle.

Two days passed. The snow continued to fall, burying the city deeper. Elara sold a few matches, but she kept the comb. She used it to brush her matted hair in the dark of her attic room. The gold teeth pulled gently, freeing tangles she had lived with for years. She looked in the cracked mirror and saw a stranger. Not a beggar, not a ghost, but a girl with clear eyes and a reflection that sparkled.

On the third day, the search began.

It started with a whisper in the baker’s shop and ended with a proclamation read by the palace herald in the central square. *Lost: A comb of dark bone, inlaid with gold and sapphire. Belonging to Her Highness, Princess Lyra. Reward: One hundred gold coins.*

One hundred gold coins. Enough to buy a house. Enough to leave Vaelis. Enough to never feel the cold again.

Elara heard the news from a group of merchants near the well. They laughed, calling the Princess a fool, saying she would search the whole city and find nothing. But Elara felt a knot in her stomach. She looked at her hands, stained with soot, and then at the comb in her pocket. It felt heavier now. It felt like a secret that was rotting inside her.

She went to the palace gates that evening. The snow was knee-deep. The guards wore fur-lined coats that made them look like bears. Elara stood at the edge of the paved courtyard, watching the search parties move through the snow with nets and rakes. They were looking for a lost object, but they were searching with the arrogance of those who believe the world is their to command.

A girl ran past her, holding a banner. *Princess Lyra Seeks Her Comb.* The letters were white against the blue silk.

Elara’s heart hammered against her ribs. She thought of the Princess. Did she cry? Did she pace? Was the comb a gift from a lover, or a memento of a dead mother? The stories said Lyra was kind, that she walked the city in disguise sometimes, eating with the common folk. But stories were lies told to make the powerful palatable.

Elara pulled the comb from her pocket. The blue stones pulsed in the dim light. She could turn it in now. She could walk up to the guard, hold it out, and say, "I found this." She would get the hundred coins. She would be rich. She would be safe.

But then she looked at her own reflection in the polished shield of a passing guard. She saw the hunger in her eyes, the desperation that had defined her for twenty years. If she took the money, she would be selling the only beautiful thing she had ever held. She would be exchanging a piece of the divine for a piece of the mundane. She would be erasing the moment she had felt, just for a second, like she belonged to a world of light rather than shadow.
