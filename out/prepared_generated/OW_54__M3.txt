On the night that Octavian, the only son of the Lord Treasurer, was born in his father’s great house of marble and porphyry, a child was likewise born to a charcoal-burner in the black alley by the river gate. The stars were out, and the moon looked down on the city of Valerius. To the rich man’s house came a Fairy clothed in silken raiment, and she stood by the cradle of Octavian and gave him the gift of Beauty. And to the hovel by the river gate she went, and stood by the pallet where the poor man’s son lay sleeping, and gave him the gift of a Kind Heart.

So the boys grew up. Octavian was fair as the morning. His skin was white, and his hair was black. He had eyes like jewels. He sat all day in the garden and looked at his face in the pool, and the servants brought him food on dishes of silver. He opened not his mouth to speak, save to cry out against the sun when it cast a shadow on the water. For he cared for nothing but his face, and his heart was as cold as the marble of his father’s house.

But the poor man’s son, whose name was Linus, went about the lanes of the city. He gave his bread to the hungry, and his cloak to the beggar. The leper did he tend. Yet the people laughed at him, for he was ill-favoured. The women turned their faces away. And the magistrates drove him from the gates, for they said that ugliness was a sign of evil.

Now when twenty years had passed, the King made a feast. And he commanded that all the young men of the city should gather at the palace gate, for his daughter sat at a window and looked at them. She was veiled, and no man had seen her face.

So Octavian put on a tunic, and shoes. He set a rose in his hair, and he went to the palace gate. And Linus the charcoal-burner’s son went also, in his rough garment, and his feet were bare.

They met at the palace gate, where the soldiers stood.

And Octavian said, ‘Why do you come here, you son of a beggar? The Princess looks for a husband, not for a cur. Go back to your alley.’

Linus answered, ‘I come because the King commanded all. And though I have no beauty, yet have I a heart, and it is a kind heart.’

‘A kind heart,’ said Octavian, and he laughed, ‘is a cloak that the poor wear. I have beauty, and beauty is a king.’

‘Yet kings die,’ said Linus, ‘and the poor endure.’

‘You speak as a fool,’ said Octavian. ‘The world is made for the beautiful. The sun shines that we may see our faces. The flowers grow that we may pluck them. You are but the earth on which I walk.’

And as he spoke there came a sound of thunder, and the sky was darkened. And the Fairy stood before them. Her raiment was red, and her eyes were bright.

She looked at Octavian and said, ‘I gave you the gift of Beauty. And what have you done with it?’

‘I have kept it,’ he answered. ‘I have not let the sun spoil it, nor the rain. It is as perfect as on the day you gave it.’

‘You have kept it,’ she said, ‘and in keeping you have lost. For beauty is a flame, and you have put a glass before it. The flame is dead, and the glass alone remains. Look at yourself.’

And Octavian looked at the pool, and he saw that his face was changed. It was as a mask. The lips were red, but they did not move. The eyes were bright, but they did not see. He was beautiful, but he was dead. And he fell to the ground.

Then the Fairy turned to Linus and said, ‘I gave you the gift of a Kind Heart. And what have you done with it?’

‘I have given it away,’ he said. ‘I gave my bread to the hungry, and my cloak to the beggar. I have nothing left.’

‘You have given it away,’ she said, ‘and in giving you have kept. For a kind heart is a well, and the more you draw from it, the fuller it grows. Look at yourself.’

And Linus looked at the pool, and he saw that his face was changed. The ugliness was gone. His eyes shone. His face was fair. And he was alive.

Then the Princess looked from the window, and she saw Linus, and she loved him. And she said to the King, ‘Father, I have found my husband. He is the one with the kind heart.’

And the King said, ‘But he is a beggar.’

‘He is a king,’ she said, ‘for he has a heart that is kind. And a kind heart is better than a crown.’

So Linus was brought into the palace, and they were married. And the King gave him a robe, and a ring of gold. And he sat at the King’s side and judged the people. He judged them with kindness, and the people loved him.

But Octavian rose from the ground, and he looked at his face in the pool, and wept. ‘I have lost my beauty,’ he cried. ‘Give it back to me, O Fairy.’

‘I cannot,’ she said. ‘You have kept it, and it is dead. Go and seek a kind heart, and perhaps you will find beauty again.’

So Octavian went into the world, and he sought a kind heart. But he found it not, for he had no kindness in him. And he wandered, and his face was as a mask. And the people said, ‘There goes the beautiful man.’ But they did not love him.
